# Annotations — The Kingdom of God Is Within You

Reader notes from Johan, kept as steer for a re-dive and for the reader edition.

## Wiener's wording (2026-10-02)

- Johan recalls that Wiener's translation turns "a Christian" into "Christianity" in Tolstoy's preface. Found 2026-10-03 (below): not in the preface but in the first sentence of chapter I, straight after it — the only such place in the book.
- Wordings that make Tolstoy political instead of religious need weighing with caution. Wiener's text is the one most readers meet: Wikipedia quotes it and Standard Ebooks publishes it (`standardebooks.org/ebooks/leo-tolstoy/the-kingdom-of-god-is-within-you/leo-wiener`). The reader edition is to use Garnett ([[feedback_translation_readability]] in memory).
- Related lead: the Christian theme dive (`docs/research/themes/christian/index.md`) records a letter of 8 August 1907 to A. Ya. Silaev preferring "a follower of Christ" to "a Christian".

## The preface: Russian, Wiener e-text and the 1904 scan (2026-10-03)

The "preface" is the eight unnumbered paragraphs before chapter I (PSS 28, pp. 1–2). Compared: the Russian (TEI, `extract_tei.py --choice=reg`), the Standard Ebooks Wiener text (`primary-sources/standard-ebooks/…_leo-wiener.epub`) and the scan of the 1904 book (`primary-sources/archive-org/TolstoyArchiveDotOrg/Complete Works/20.pdf`, printed pp. 3–4), read from the page images. The project has no machine translation or local Garnett for this work yet, so the literal glosses below are ours.

- **"A Christian" is kept.** «не допускают для христианина употребления оружия» — Wiener: "who do not admit for a Christian the use of weapons", in the scan and the e-text alike.
- **"What I believe" becomes "what my religion is".** «В книге этой я действительно изложил то, во что я верю» (literal: "In this book I really set out what I believe"). Wiener: "In this book I really expounded what my religion is." The book's title «В чем моя вера?» ("What Is My Faith?") is "My Religion", the title the 1885 English translation had already given it. Belief turned into religion, twice in the first two sentences.
- **"Departures from Christ's teaching" becomes "deviations of this teaching of Christ".** «В числе многих отступлений этого учения от учения Христа» (literal: "Among the many departures of this [church] teaching from the teaching of Christ"). Wiener drops the "from", so the sentence reads as if Christ's teaching had the deviations, not the Church's.
- **"By violence" is dropped.** «непризнание заповеди непротивления злу насилием» (literal: "the failure to acknowledge the commandment of non-resistance to evil by violence"). Wiener: "the commandment of non-resistance to evil". Tolstoy gives the full formula here, at the point where he names the chief departure; without "by violence" it reads as passivity toward evil, which he did not teach. (Later in the preface the Russian itself shortens it to «непротивлении злу».)
- **"Church teaching" becomes "church doctrine".** «извращение церковным учением учения Христа» — Wiener: "the distortion of Christ's teaching in the church doctrine". Minor.
- **Epigraph.** «не делайтесь рабами человеков» ("do not become slaves of men") — Wiener uses the King James wording, "servants of men".

Every Wiener wording above matches the 1904 scan, so all of them are Wiener's own choices, not transcription errors. Two differences between scan and e-text, neither touching the wording: the 1904 book misprints the first epigraph as "John viii. 23" (it is 8:32; Standard Ebooks corrected it), and the heading "Preface" is Standard Ebooks' addition — neither the 1904 book nor the Russian has one.

## "A Christian" through the whole book (2026-10-03)

Every Russian sentence with «христианин» in the singular (60 sentences, 73 uses, chapters I–XII) was lined up with Wiener's text (Standard Ebooks). Everywhere but one, Wiener has "a Christian" (or "every Christian", "a Christian's"). The one exception is the first sentence of chapter I:

- Russian: «выражая свое сочувствие моим взглядам о незаконности для христианина всякого насилия и войны» (literal: "expressing their sympathy with my views on the unlawfulness *for a Christian* of all violence and war").
- Wiener: "which express their sympathy with my views concerning the unlawfulness *for Christianity* of all violence and war".

The 1904 scan (vol. XX, printed p. 5) has the same words, so it is Wiener's own wording, not a transcription error. The shift matters: Tolstoy speaks of what a person may not do; Wiener's wording makes it a position of a religion. Two sentences later the same paragraph has «для христианина обязанность» and Wiener gets it right: "the obligation for a Christian".

The search did not cover the plural «христиане» or Wiener's other volumes.

## "By violence" through the whole book (2026-10-03)

The Russian names the commandment «непротивление злу насилием» ("non-resistance to evil by violence") 40 times. None of Wiener's 37 uses of "nonresistance to evil" carries "by violence". He keeps it only where Tolstoy uses the verb ("resisting evil with violence", "by means of violence", about a dozen times). So the short name — which reads as doing nothing about evil — is Wiener's throughout, starting in the preface (above) and the same first paragraph of chapter I, where «учение Христа о непротивлении злу насилием» is "Christ's teaching about non-resistance to evil" (scan p. 5).

## Garnett at the same places (2026-10-03)

Constance Garnett's 1894 translation (Project Gutenberg #43302; local copy `primary-sources/project-gutenberg/leo-tolstoy_the-kingdom-of-god-is-within-you_constance-garnett.epub`, plus `.txt`), the planned reading text, checked at the places above:

- **Preface:** "a book under the title 'What I Believe,' in which I did in fact make a sincere statement of my beliefs" — belief stays belief, and the title is translated, not "My Religion". "Among the many points in which this doctrine falls short of the doctrine of Christ" — the fault stays with the Church's teaching. "the absence of any commandment of non-resistance to evil by force" — "by force" kept, though «непризнание» ("non-recognition") becomes "absence". "who do not allow a Christian the use of weapons".
- **Chapter I, first sentence:** "my views on the unlawfulness for a Christian of war and the use of force" — "a Christian" kept.
- **"By force":** 25 of Garnett's 42 uses of "non-resistance to evil" carry "by force", against none of Wiener's 37; several of the bare ones are where the Russian is bare too (e.g. preface paras 4 and 8). Not yet checked one by one.

## Maude at the same places (2026-10-03)

Aylmer Maude's translation (*The Kingdom of God and Peace Essays*, Oxford University Press 1936, Tolstoy Centenary Edition) is on the Internet Archive as two openly readable Digital Library of India scans: `in.ernet.dli.2015.461988` (labelled public domain) and `in.ernet.dli.2015.77097` (a later Geoffrey Cumberlege printing, labelled in copyright); also lending-only copies of 1942 and 1960. Not saved locally. Maude died in 1938, so the text is public domain in the UK and EU; its US status was not checked. Read from the text of `461988`, with the preface and chapter I opening checked against the page images (printed pp. 445–447).

- **Preface:** "a book called *What I Believe*, in which I gave an account of what I do believe"; "Among the many divergences of that doctrine from the teaching of Christ"; "its omission to acknowledge the law of non-resistance to evil by violence"; "who do not allow a Christian to use weapons". All four points right — and «непризнание» rendered as "omission to acknowledge", closer than Garnett's "absence".
- **Chapter I, first sentence:** "my views as to the unlawfulness of war or the use of violence for a Christian".
- **"By violence":** across the whole book, "evil by violence/force" occurs 13 times in Wiener, 21 in Maude and 39 in Garnett, against 40 uses of «злу насилием» in the Russian. Maude often shortens to plain "non-resistance" (his introduction says Tolstoy himself used "Resist not evil" and "non-resistance" as short forms), but the bare long form "non-resistance to evil" appears only seven times, two of them where the Russian is bare too (preface paras 4 and 8). Not yet checked one by one.
- **Epigraph:** «рабами человеков» is "bondservants of men" (Revised Version wording) — closer to "slaves" than Wiener's "servants". Maude's printing also misprints the first reference, as "John iii. 32" (Wiener's has "John viii. 23").

## The Swedish translation: Hedberg 1894 (2026-10-03)

*Frälsningen finnes hos dig själf, eller Kristendomen framställd icke som en mystisk troslära, utan som en ny sedelag*, translated by Walborg Hedberg (Stockholm: Albert Bonniers förlag, printed by Alb. Bonniers boktryckeri, 1894). Johan's photos of the Malmö City Library copy (shelfmark Cf, no. 262542; rebound, spine "Frälsningen finnes hos dig själv"): `_resources/The Kingdom of God is Within you/Frälsningen finnes hos dig själf (1894)/` — spine, title page, epigraphs + preface opening, rest of the preface. The only Swedish edition Johan could find in the library; the copy is in very poor condition and held together with tape. A witness for the re-dive, not a reading text.

Johan's notes:

- **The title.** «Царство божие внутри вас» ("The kingdom of God is within you") becomes *Frälsningen finnes hos dig själf* ("Salvation is to be found in yourself"). Calling the kingdom of God "salvation" is a stretch, to say the least. The literal words survive only as a motto under the title: "Guds rike är inom eder. (Luk. 17:21.)"
- **The subtitle.** «Христианство не как мистическое учение, а как новое жизнепонимание» (literal: "Christianity not as a mystical teaching but as a new understanding of life") becomes "Kristendomen framställd icke som en mystisk troslära, utan som en ny sedelag" ("Christianity presented not as a mystical doctrine of faith but as a new moral law"). Both choices blur the line between a personal faith and organised religion (the Church): *troslära* is the word for a church's body of doctrine, and *sedelag* turns an understanding of life — something inward — into a code of rules. For comparison, the English for «учение» / «жизнепонимание»: Wiener "teaching" / "concept of life", Garnett "religion" / "theory of life", Maude "doctrine" / "understanding of life".
- **An abridged text.** The preface carries a translator's footnote saying she cut and shortened the book. Translated: "In rendering the present work in Swedish, the translator, in view of the book's great length and occasional long-windedness, and in the hope of doing the reader a service, without failing in the reverence due to the author, has made some omissions and abridgements — naturally only where this could be done without doing violence to the meaning and the context. The abridgements concern only repetitions of the same argument, and the omissions a couple of non-essential digressions from the subject and some longer quotations from other writers, meant to further illustrate the propositions Tolstoy himself preaches with greater eloquence, and which were therefore judged safe to leave out." Original: «Vid återgifvandet på svenska af föreliggande arbete har öfvers., i anseende till bokens stora bredd och stundtals förekommande omständlighet samt i hopp att göra läsaren en tjänst, utan att brista i skyldig pietet mot författaren, vidtagit några uteslutningar och förkortningar, naturligtvis endast där så kunnat ske utan att förgripa sig på meningen och sammanhanget. Förkortningarna ha endast gällt upprepningar af samma argument, och uteslutningarna ett par oväsentliga utvikningar från ämnet samt några längre citat af andra skriftställare, afsedda att ytterligare belysa de satser, Tolstoy själf predikar med större vältalighet, och som därför ansetts saklöst kunna utelämnas.» The "longer quotations from other writers" are likely the backbone of chapters I–II (Garrison, Ballou, Dymond, Chelčický, the critics) — to be checked.

The preface, set against the Russian (photos 3–4):

- **Belief.** The title «В чем моя вера?» is "Hvari min tro består" ("What my faith consists of") — closer than Wiener's *My Religion*. But «то, во что я верю» ("what I believe") becomes "alla mina trossatser" ("all my articles of faith"), and «свою веру в учение Христа» ("my faith in Christ's teaching") becomes "mitt sätt att förstå Kristi lära" ("my way of understanding Christ's teaching").
- **"Error" becomes "heresy", and the sentence may be reversed.** «я не мог не высказать и того, почему я не верю и считаю заблуждением ту церковную веру, которая обыкновенно называется христианством» ("I could not help also stating why I do not believe in, and consider an error, the church faith usually called Christianity"). Hedberg: "nämnde jag dock icke, hvarför jag som ett kätteri betraktar den officiella religion, som bär namnet kristendom". As printed it reads "I did *not* mention why I regard as a *heresy* the official religion that bears the name Christianity" — the opposite of «не мог не» ("could not help"), perhaps a misread double negative. *Kätteri* ("heresy") is the Church's own word for departing from its doctrine, so it is turned back on the Church; Tolstoy wrote «заблуждение» ("error, delusion"). "Den officiella religion" for «церковную веру» ("the church faith") keeps the Church in view. Open: does "nämnde jag dock icke" really read as a plain negation in 1890s Swedish? Johan's ear decides.
- **The departure from Christ and "by violence" are kept.** "Den skiljer sig … från Kristi lära i många fall, hvaribland jag först och främst lagt märke till uteslutandet af det bud, som förbjuder oss att motstå det onda med våld" ("It differs … from Christ's teaching in many cases, among which I first of all noted the exclusion of the commandment that forbids us to resist evil with violence"). Direction right (the Church departs from Christ) and «насилием» kept as "med våld"; «непризнание» ("non-recognition") becomes "uteslutandet" ("the exclusion"), like Garnett's "absence".
- **"A Christian" is kept.** «не допускают для христианина употребления оружия» → "icke anse det tillständigt för en kristen att bära vapen" ("do not consider it permissible for a Christian to bear arms").
- **Shortened already in the preface.** The book's spread «в рукописях и литографиях в России и в переводах за границей» ("in manuscripts and lithographs in Russia and in translations abroad") becomes "vann skriften dock stor framgång i Ryssland, och talrika öfversättningar gjordes däraf utomlands" ("the work nonetheless won great success in Russia, and numerous translations were made abroad"). The critics «духовными, т. е. исповедующими христианскую религию … так и светскими, т. е. не исповедующими христианскую религию» ("clerical, i.e. professing the Christian religion … and secular, i.e. not professing it") become "präster och lekmän" ("priests and laymen"), dropping Tolstoy's own definitions.
- **Epigraphs.** «рабами человеков» is "människors trälar" ("thralls of men") — closer to "slaves" than Wiener's "servants" or Maude's "bondservants" — and the first reference is printed correctly as "Joh. 8:32", unlike the 1904 Wiener and 1936 Maude printings.
