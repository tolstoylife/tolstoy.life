# Annotations — The Kingdom of God Is Within You

Reader notes from Johan, kept as steer for a re-dive and for the reader edition.

## Wiener's wording (2026-10-02)

- Johan recalls that Wiener's translation turns "a Christian" into "Christianity" in Tolstoy's preface. Found 2026-10-03 (below): not in the preface but in the first sentence of chapter I, straight after it — the only such place in the book.
- Wordings that make Tolstoy political instead of religious need weighing with caution. Wiener's text is the one most readers meet: Wikipedia quotes it and Standard Ebooks publishes it (`standardebooks.org/ebooks/leo-tolstoy/the-kingdom-of-god-is-within-you/leo-wiener`). The reader edition is to use Garnett ([[feedback_translation_readability]] in memory).
- Related lead: the Christian theme dive (`docs/research/themes/christian/index.md`) records a letter of 8 August 1907 to A. Ya. Silaev preferring "a follower of Christ" to "a Christian".

## The preface: Russian, Wiener e-text and the 1904 scan (2026-10-03)

The "preface" is the eight unnumbered paragraphs before chapter I (PSS 28, pp. 1–2). Compared: the Russian (TEI, `extract_tei.py --choice=reg`), the Standard Ebooks Wiener text (`primary-sources/standard-ebooks/…_leo-wiener.epub`) and the scan of the 1904 book (`primary-sources/archive-org/TolstoyArchiveDotOrg/Complete Works/20.pdf`, printed pp. 3–4), read from the page images. The project has no machine translation or local Garnett for this work yet, so the literal glosses below are ours.

- **"A Christian" is kept.** «не допускают для христианина употребления оружия» — Wiener: "who do not admit for a Christian the use of weapons", in the scan and the e-text alike.
- **"What I believe" becomes "what my religion is".** «В книге этой я действительно изложил то, во что я верю» (literal: "In this book I really set out what I believe"). Wiener: "In this book I really expounded what my religion is." The book's title «В чем моя вера?» ("What Is My Faith?") is "My Religion", the title the 1885 English translation had already given it. Belief turned into religion, twice in the first two sentences.
- **"Departures from Christ's teaching" becomes "deviations of this teaching of Christ".** «В числе многих отступлений этого учения от учения Христа» (literal: "Among the many departures of this [church] teaching from the teaching of Christ"). Wiener drops the "from", so the sentence reads as if Christ's teaching had the deviations, not the Church's.
- **"By violence" is dropped.** «непризнание заповеди непротивления злу насилием» (literal: "the failure to acknowledge the commandment of non-resistance to evil by violence"). Wiener: "the commandment of non-resistance to evil". Tolstoy gives the full formula here, at the point where he names the chief departure; without "by violence" it reads as passivity toward evil, which he did not teach. (Later in the preface the Russian itself shortens it to «непротивлении злу».)
- **"Church teaching" becomes "church doctrine".** «извращение церковным учением учения Христа» — Wiener: "the distortion of Christ's teaching in the church doctrine". Minor.
- **Epigraph.** «не делайтесь рабами человеков» ("do not become slaves of men") — Wiener uses the King James wording, "servants of men".

Every Wiener wording above matches the 1904 scan, so all of them are Wiener's own choices, not transcription errors. Two differences between scan and e-text, neither touching the wording: the 1904 book misprints the first epigraph as "John viii. 23" (it is 8:32; Standard Ebooks corrected it), and the heading "Preface" is Standard Ebooks' addition — neither the 1904 book nor the Russian has one.

## "A Christian" through the whole book (2026-10-03)

Every Russian sentence with «христианин» in the singular (60 sentences, 73 uses, chapters I–XII) was lined up with Wiener's text (Standard Ebooks). Everywhere but one, Wiener has "a Christian" (or "every Christian", "a Christian's"). The one exception is the first sentence of chapter I:

- Russian: «выражая свое сочувствие моим взглядам о незаконности для христианина всякого насилия и войны» (literal: "expressing their sympathy with my views on the unlawfulness *for a Christian* of all violence and war").
- Wiener: "which express their sympathy with my views concerning the unlawfulness *for Christianity* of all violence and war".

The 1904 scan (vol. XX, printed p. 5) has the same words, so it is Wiener's own wording, not a transcription error. The shift matters: Tolstoy speaks of what a person may not do; Wiener's wording makes it a position of a religion. Two sentences later the same paragraph has «для христианина обязанность» and Wiener gets it right: "the obligation for a Christian".

The search did not cover the plural «христиане» or Wiener's other volumes.

## "By violence" through the whole book (2026-10-03)

The Russian names the commandment «непротивление злу насилием» ("non-resistance to evil by violence") 40 times. None of Wiener's 37 uses of "nonresistance to evil" carries "by violence". He keeps it only where Tolstoy uses the verb ("resisting evil with violence", "by means of violence", about a dozen times). So the short name — which reads as doing nothing about evil — is Wiener's throughout, starting in the preface (above) and the same first paragraph of chapter I, where «учение Христа о непротивлении злу насилием» is "Christ's teaching about non-resistance to evil" (scan p. 5).

## Garnett at the same places (2026-10-03)

Constance Garnett's 1894 translation (Project Gutenberg #43302; no local copy yet), the planned reading text, checked at the places above:

- **Preface:** "a book under the title 'What I Believe,' in which I did in fact make a sincere statement of my beliefs" — belief stays belief, and the title is translated, not "My Religion". "Among the many points in which this doctrine falls short of the doctrine of Christ" — the fault stays with the Church's teaching. "the absence of any commandment of non-resistance to evil by force" — "by force" kept, though «непризнание» ("non-recognition") becomes "absence". "who do not allow a Christian the use of weapons".
- **Chapter I, first sentence:** "my views on the unlawfulness for a Christian of war and the use of force" — "a Christian" kept.
- **"By force":** 25 of Garnett's 42 uses of "non-resistance to evil" carry "by force", against none of Wiener's 37; several of the bare ones are where the Russian is bare too (e.g. preface paras 4 and 8). Not yet checked one by one.

Maude's own translation of this book (*The Kingdom of God and Peace Essays*, 1936) is not held locally and has not been compared.
