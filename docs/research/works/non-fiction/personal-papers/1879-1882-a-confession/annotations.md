# Annotations — A Confession

Johan's 45 reader notes on the Wiener reader edition (`confession.en-wiener`, chapters I–VI), made 24–27 September 2026 on research.tolstoy.life and sent through the site's Send button on 1 October. They are interpretive steer for wiki ingestion and open questions — kept out of the bare dive. They do not change the dive's verified findings.

The notes are anchored by paragraph and sentence id (`p-3-2-s3` = chapter III, paragraph 2, sentence 3). The paragraph part is shared by every edition in the bundle, so each note points to the same place in the Maude and Russian texts; the sentence number is Wiener's own and can differ by one or two in Maude.

## Outcome: the reader edition moved to Maude

The notes ended with the decision to replace Wiener with **Aylmer Maude's 1921 translation** (Standard Ebooks) as the bundle's English reading text, with its own read-along audio. Three of the notes (I.5, I.6, IV.4) say Wiener's sentences were hard to follow, and that the project's machine translation read more easily — the reason for the switch. Most of the remaining "typo" notes were defects in the Wiener e-text and do not carry over (see the end of this page).

## Key sentences (Johan's marks)

- **III.5** "…which was that I should teach not knowing what." — "Key sentence and theme of the change."
- **III.11** "In my writings I advocated … the greatest comfort for oneself and one's family." — "Very illuminating of the difference between the novel period and the prophet period." A frankness one likely has to be born again to admit. No fix needed (the note was tagged `needs-fix` by mistake).
- **IV.3** "The truth was that life was meaningless." — key sentence.
- **V.4** that science's silence was "the fault of my own ignorance" — "I recognize this feeling."
- **V.10** the "law of evolution … I am ashamed to confess it" passage — "Been there myself, and you really feel silly for believing all the nonsense, like math being able to explain anything."
- **V.11** the definiteness of the sciences "in inverse proportion to their applicability to the questions of life" — "Bullseye."
- **V.16** "Experimental science gives positive knowledge … only when it does not introduce the final cause" — "Good summary."
- **VI.6** "You are an accidentally cohering globule of something." — "Very poignant summary of experimental science."
- **VI.13** "For in much wisdom is much grief: and he that increaseth knowledge increaseth sorrow" — "Very true."
- **VI.28** "Everything is vanity" — the low point before finding faith.

- **III.2 — "I with my heart was the judge"**: heart symbolism. Check how *heart* compares with *soul* and *conscience* across his writing — a possible theme dive.
- **VI.9 — jurisprudence**: gloss as "the science of law".
- **II.14 — "everything which exists is reasonable"** (Hegel's dictum): "the praise of the status quo — perhaps only culture?" Worth a gloss naming the source.

## Meaning questions (Wiener's wording)

- **II.1** "a large number of slaves" — "perhaps a common wish?" The Russian is «рабов»; Maude has "serfs".
- **III.1** "borne in a bark by the waves" — "?" (a *bark* is a boat; Maude: "carried somewhere").
- **III.12** "by minutes at first of perplexity" — meaning? (Maude: "moments of perplexity").
- **V.15** "fife of all humanity" — an OCR error for *life*.
- **I.5** "If you come in contact with it…" — what is "it"? (the religious teaching).

## Pronunciation (audio)

- **I.3** "1838" was read "one thousand…" — `reader/speech.py` now speaks 18xx/19xx years as years.
- **VI.10** Schopenhauer and **VI.12** *Wille zum Leben* — respelled for the voice in `reader/speech.py`.
- **II.5** the pause after "St." (Wiener's "St. Petersburg") — Maude writes "Petersburg", so it does not arise.
