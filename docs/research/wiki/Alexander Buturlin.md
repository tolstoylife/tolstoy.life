Alexander Sergeevich Buturlin (<span lang="ru">Александр Сергеевич Бутурлин</span>) was a physician and an old acquaintance. He appears in the record of [[The Great Sin]] once, in the diary entry that also notes the start of its writing.

## The visit of 20 April 1905

On 20 April 1905 Buturlin went with Tolstoy to the peasant Pyotr Osipov (<span lang="ru">Пётр Осипов</span>). The diary for 21 April records it: "Yesterday, with Buturlin, I was at Pyotr Osipov's, and he harshly reproached me for the fact that I speak [against it] yet buy up land. It was both painful and good."[^1] The same entry begins "Began writing: The People's Defenders. Not bad. And Henry George", the two pieces that became the essay.[^2]

Buturlin was the companion on the visit, not the one who reproached. The dive links the reproach to the essay's close, which speaks in the first person: "we, Russian parasites, nourished and given leisure for mental work by the labours of the people, shall understand our sin".[^3]
