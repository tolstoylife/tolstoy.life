Aleksandr Sergeevich Buturlin (А. С. Бутурлин) was a physician and an old acquaintance of Tolstoy. He enters [[The Great Sin]] through a single day in its genesis.

## The reproach he witnessed

On 20 April 1905 Buturlin went with Tolstoy to visit the peasant Pyotr Osipov, who — to Tolstoy's face — reproached him for preaching against landowning while himself buying land. The diary of the next day records it: «Вчера с Бутурлиным был у Петра Осипова, и он жестко упрекал меня за то, что я говорю, а скупаю землю. Было и больно и хорошо» — "Yesterday, with Buturlin, I was at Pyotr Osipov's, and he harshly reproached me for the fact that I speak [against it] yet buy up land. It was both painful and good" (<abbr title="Полное собрание сочинений — the 90-volume Jubilee edition of the complete works">PSS</abbr> 55:135–137). It was the same day Tolstoy began writing the essay.

The reproach is not incidental. The first-person «мы, русские паразиты» — we Russian parasites — of the essay's close is this rebuke absorbed and turned on the author himself. Buturlin's part is small — the companion on the visit, not the reproacher — but it fixes the episode as an event, not a rhetorical device.
