<span lang="ru">Василий Кириллович Сютаев</span> was a peasant whom Tolstoy visited at Torzhok in the autumn of 1881, in the years he was writing [[A Confession]] (1879–82). He appears in the diary and letters of the move to Moscow, beside [[Nikolai Fyodorov]], as one of the few people Tolstoy found living by the teaching he was then working out.

## The 1881 visit

The diary entry of 5 October 1881 (OS), which opens on the move to Moscow as the most painful month of his life, ends: "Was at Torzhok at Sutaev's, a consolation."[^1]

In a letter of mid to late November 1881 to [[Vasily Alekseev]], Tolstoy names the people he has found in Moscow, Fyodorov first among them, and then adds: "I also went to see Sutaev. Also a Christian, and in deed." Of his gospel synthesis (the Short Exposition of the Gospel) he writes that Orlov and Fyodorov had read it and that they were of one mind; "with Sutaev, in everything, down to the smallest details."[^2]

## Later mention

In a long letter to [[Mikhail Engelhardt]] of late 1882 or early 1883, arguing that education adds nothing to a person, Tolstoy points to Sutaev: "an illiterate peasant", whose influence on people and on the intelligentsia was "greater and more significant than that of all Russian scholars and writers".[^3]
