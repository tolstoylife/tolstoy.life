Dushan Makovitsky (Dušan Makovický; in the Russian sources «Д. П. Маковицкий») was Tolstoy's resident physician and the diarist of the household. His «Яснополянские записки» (Yasnaya Polyana Notes) are the day-by-day record the
<abbr title="Полное собрание сочинений — the 90-volume Jubilee edition of the complete works">PSS</abbr>
commentary to [[The Great Sin]] leans on for its dating.

## What his notes date

The commentary reconstructs the essay's composition largely from Makovitsky:

- **28 April 1905** — his notes record that Tolstoy read *The Great Sin* aloud to several people that day.
- **1 July 1905** — he records that Tolstoy gave the finished article to his daughter [[Maria Tolstaya]] (Maria Lvovna Obolenskaya) to copy for *Русская мысль*, and meant to take it to the post himself.
- **11 July 1905** — he records the proofs arriving.

Without the Notes these dates would be lost; they are why the genesis can be told day by day (PSS 36 commentary, pp. 665–666).

## The doctor in Chapter VII

The commentary makes a quieter identification. The scene at Tula station in Chapter VII — where a newspaper article is read aloud and an old peasant, understanding none of the jargon, grasps only that a great and ancient sin is meant and crosses himself — turns on a real reading, and the reader was Makovitsky. What he actually read out was not, as the essay's telling has it, an article on the "agrarian" congress, but Tolstoy's *own* piece «О разрешении земельного вопроса по проекту Генри Джорджа» — an extract from [[To the Working People]] — printed in *Вечерняя почта* No. 117 on 20 April 1905 (commentary, keyed to the *Yasnaya Polyana Notes* entry of 25 April 1905). The essay's most-quoted moment thus has a documented occasion, and the doctor at the centre of it.
