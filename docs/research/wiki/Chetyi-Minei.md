The *Chetyi-Minei* (<span lang="ru">Четьи-Минеи</span>) is "a collection of the lives of the saints of the Orthodox Church".[^1] The lives are arranged by the days of the year, for reading. Maude translates the name "the Lives of Holy men"; Wiener, "the menaions".

## In A Confession

In chapter XIV, after he drew near to the people and their faith, Tolstoy writes: "So also was it when I read the Lives of Holy men, which became my favourite books. Putting aside the miracles and regarding them as fables illustrating thoughts, this reading revealed to me life's meaning" (XIV.7).[^2] The Russian names two books, the *Chetyi-Minei* and the *Prologues*.

The PSS commentary gives the editions:

- **The *Chetyi-Minei*.** He read the *Book of the Lives of the Saints* printed at the Synodal Press in Moscow, fifth printing, 1864, in 12 volumes; his copy, with many of his marks, is in the library at Yasnaya Polyana. He also read the *Great Menaion Reader* collected by Metropolitan Makary, and V. Klyuchevsky's study *Old Russian Saints' Lives as a Historical Source* (Moscow 1871), whose Yasnaya Polyana copy is also heavily marked.[^1]
- **The *Prologue*.** A widely read book of saints' lives and sermons in old Russia; the oldest copy is of the 12th century and the first printed edition of 1642–44. He read the 1875–76 edition; his marked copy is at Yasnaya Polyana, and its bookmarks suggest he last read it in June 1880. The commentary places the readings named in XIV.7 in the *Prologue*: the life of Macarius the Great (19 January), the life of Prince Joasaph, a Christian retelling of the legend of the [[Buddha]] (18 November), the parable of the traveller in the well (19 November), the monk who found gold (13 September), Peter the publican (22 September), and the words of [[John Chrysostom]], scattered through the whole book.[^3]

## Afterwards

In 1884–85, Birukoff writes, Tolstoy worked up popular legends for the Posrednik publishing house, "with selections from the *Chetyi-Minei* and the *Prologues*".[^4] One result is *Sufferings of the Saints* (1885), two lives of martyrs adapted from the *Chetyi-Minei* of Dmitry of Rostov ([stories-for-the-people dive](../themes/stories-for-the-people/index.md)).[^5]

## Open questions

- The PSS names the 1864 Moscow edition as the one Tolstoy read; the dive summary of *Sufferings of the Saints* names Dmitry of Rostov's compilation. Whether the 1864 printing is Dmitry's text is not checked here.
- Which lives Tolstoy marked in his copies is not shown by these sources.
