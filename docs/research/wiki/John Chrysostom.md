John Chrysostom (<span lang="ru">Иоанн Златоуст</span>; c. 347–407) was a Church Father and Archbishop of Constantinople, known for his sermons. The name means "golden-mouthed".

## In A Confession

[[A Confession]] (<span lang="ru">Исповедь</span>, 1879–82) names him once, among the readings in which Tolstoy found the meaning of life after he drew near to the people: "There were the lives of Makarius the Great, the story of Buddha, there were the words of St. John Chrysostom" (XIV.7).[^1]

The PSS commentary places these readings in the *Prologue* (<span lang="ru">Пролог</span>), a book of saints' lives and sermons, where "the words of John Chrysostom are scattered in great number through the whole book". Tolstoy read the *Prologue* in the 1875–76 edition; his copy, with many of his marks, is in the library at Yasnaya Polyana, and its bookmarks suggest he last read it in June 1880.[^2] See also [[Buddha]], whose story as Prince Joasaph is in the same book.

## Open questions

- Which of Chrysostom's sermons Tolstoy marked in his copy of the *Prologue* is not shown by these sources.
- His dates and the first sentence are general knowledge. His Tolstoy Digital person entry (id 5792) is referenced in the TEI text but missing from our copy of the person list.
- He is also named twice in *The Kingdom of God Is Within You* (chapters III and IV); that dive has not been drawn on here.
