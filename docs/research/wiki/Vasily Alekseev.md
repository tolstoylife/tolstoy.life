Vasily Ivanovich Alekseev (<span lang="ru">Василий Иванович Алексеев</span>) was tutor to the Tolstoy children.[^1] He was a correspondent in the years when [[A Confession]] (1879–82) was written, and the recipient of a letter of November 1881 that describes the first months in Moscow and the people met there.

## The letter of November 1881

The editors of the <abbr title="Полное собрание сочинений — the 90-volume Jubilee edition of the complete works">PSS</abbr> date the letter to 15–30 November 1881, from Moscow, with a query.[^2] It opens: "Things are very hard for me in Moscow."[^3] The letter compares Alekseev's own experience: he had known Kansas from geography but came to know it only on arrival there.[^4]

The letter weighs ways out: preaching by word and print, or doing good to people. The only way it sees is "to live well — always to turn one's good side to everyone", and it adds that this is not yet within reach, as it is for Alekseev.[^5] It then names the people met in Moscow: Orlov, and above all [[Nikolai Fyodorov]], librarian of the Rumyantsev library. A visit to [[Vasily Sutaev]] is reported. Orlov and Fyodorov had read the *Brief Exposition* (the gospel synthesis), and "we are of one mind"; with Sutaev, "in everything".[^6] The letter closes with greetings to Lizaveta Aleksandrovna, Liza and Kolya.[^7]

## Later letters

The dive's sweep of PSS volume 63 lists further letters to Alekseev, which were not extracted. A joint letter to A. A. Bibikov and Alekseev of 1–5 September 1881 marks the arrival in Moscow.[^8] A letter of July 1884 calls Alekseev the first person "touched by education" whom Tolstoy knew to profess the new faith "not in words, but in the heart".[^9]
