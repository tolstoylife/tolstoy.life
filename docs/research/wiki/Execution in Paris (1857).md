On 6 April 1857 (OS: 25 March), during his first journey abroad, Tolstoy watched a man put to death by guillotine in Paris. The <abbr title="Полное собрание сочинений — the 90-volume Jubilee edition of the complete works">PSS</abbr> commentary to [[Confession]] dates it and says only that the man executed was a murderer (PSS 23 commentary, note 13). The name of the condemned man is NEEDS PRIMARY SOURCE.

## What A Confession says

In chapter III, the execution is the first of two cases where, against the "faith in progress" he lived by in those years, «не разум, а чувство возмущалось» — not reason but feeling rebelled. «Так, в бытность мою в Париже, вид смертной казни обличил мне шаткость моего суеверия прогресса» (PSS 23:8). In Maude's words: "When I saw the head part from the body and how they thumped separately into the box, I understood, not with my mind but with my whole being, that no theory of the reasonableness of our present progress could justify this deed; and that though everybody from the creation of the world had held it to be necessary, on whatever theory, I knew it to be unnecessary and bad; and therefore the arbiter of what is good and evil is not what people say and do, nor is it progress, but it is my heart and I." The Russian ends «а я с своим сердцем» — "but I with my heart" (working English).

The second case, in the same paragraph, is the slow death of his brother (Nikolai Nikolaevich, d. 1860, per the commentary's note 14). Both are framed as "rare instances of doubt": he says he went on living by the faith in progress all the same.

## The diary entry

The commentary quotes the diary for that day (PSS 47:121–122, as given in PSS 23 commentary, note 13): «Больной, встал в 7 часов и поехал смотреть экзекуцию. Толстая белая здоровая шея и грудь. Целовал Евангелие, и потом — смерть, что за бессмыслица! — Сильное и не даром прошедшее впечатление. Я не политический человек. Мораль и искусство. Я знаю, люблю и могу... Гильотина долго не давала спать и заставляла оглядываться» — Ill, got up at 7 and went to see the execution. A thick, white, healthy neck and chest. He kissed the Gospel, and then — death; what senselessness! — A strong impression, and not one that passed for nothing. I am not a political man. Morality and art. I know, love and can… The guillotine kept me from sleep for a long time and made me keep looking round (working English).

The same note points to two other places he wrote about it: a letter to V. P. Botkin the same day (PSS 60:167–169) and chapter II of *What Then Must We Do?* (PSS 25:190). Neither is in this dive's extracts.

## How it entered the book

The episode is not in the earliest surviving redaction, which lacks both the Paris execution and the brother's death (PSS 23 commentary). On a later full copy (manuscript 3 in the commentary's list) a margin plan-note reads «В это время гильотина. Что-то екнуло, но ничего не нашел и продолжал жить по-старому, только стал искать в массах народа» — At that time, the guillotine. Something gave a jolt, but I found nothing and went on living the old way, only began to search among the mass of the people (working English). The passage itself first appears in the first author's proof, together with the brother's death (PSS 23 commentary).

An earlier wording printed among the variants (PSS 23:488–511) shares the first half of the final sentence but draws a different conclusion. Where the final text says no theory of progress can justify the deed and the judge is «я с своим сердцем», the draft says he understood «что люди сделали ужасное, ничем не извиняемое дело, что я участник этого дела хоть тем, что я не сделал всего, что мог, чтобы помешать этому» — that people had done a terrible deed with no excuse, and that he was a participant in it, if only in not having done all he could to prevent it (working English). The draft also has people hiding from themselves «отсутствие всякой веры» (the absence of any faith) where the final text has «свое непонимание жизни» (their lack of understanding of life). Our reading: the draft makes the point one of his own complicity; the final text makes it one of where moral judgment comes from.
