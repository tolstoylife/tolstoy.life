# Annotations — Bethink Yourselves!

Reader notes from Johan on the 1904 English reader edition (`docs/reader/non-fiction/essays-and-criticism/bethink-yourselves/`, version `en-1904`), kept as steer for a re-dive and for the reader edition. Interpretive steer, not text for a page.

## Notes on sections 9–10 (2026-11-22)

- **"Chi-nam-po" (p-9-2-s7) is the translator's place, not Tolstoy's.** The Russian has «отстоял ли я Юнампо с его лесными складами» ("whether I defended Yongampo with its timber stores"). Yongampo is the small port at the mouth of the Yalu where the Russian timber company had built its depots in 1903, one of the disputes that led to the war. Chinampo (today Nampo) is a different Korean port, near Pyongyang. The 1904 English names the wrong one. The background on Yongampo and the timber company is general historical knowledge, not checked against a source here. Reader edition: footnote 1 at p-9-2-s7 gives the Russian name (added 2026-11-22).
- **"Religious" is a good word for Tolstoy (p-9-7-s1).** «человек, понимающий назначение своей жизни, то есть человек религиозный» ("a man who understands the purpose of his life, that is, a religious man"). The 1904 English is faithful. Johan's reading: religion here is the faith of ordinary people, not the institutions. For wiki pages: don't let "religion" or "religious" slide toward the churches when Tolstoy uses them this way.
- **"Enemies" are whole peoples (p-10-3-s1).** «Любить врагов, японцев, китайцев, тех желтых людей, к которым заблудшие люди теперь стараются возбудить в нас ненависть» ("To love enemies, the Japanese, the Chinese, those yellow people toward whom misguided men are now trying to stir up hatred in us"). Johan's reading: the enemies are not mainly the people around you whom you might dislike, but the peoples we are taught we must defend ourselves against.
- **The crimes named are Western, not only Russian (p-10-3-s1).** The same sentence names the English (opium), the French, Russians and Germans (seizing land), and the Russians (burying people alive for damaging «дороги», "the road" — probably the railway, tying them together by their pigtails, drowning them in the Amur). Johan's reading: here Tolstoy goes outside Russia to point at the crimes of the Western states. Two details for the edition: the 1904 English "their river Amur" adds "their" (the Russian is just «в Амуре», "in the Amur"); and «связывать косами» is "tying together by their braids", i.e. the Chinese queue. The drownings in the Amur are generally taken to be the Blagoveshchensk killings of Chinese residents in July 1900; that link is consensus, not read from a source here.
