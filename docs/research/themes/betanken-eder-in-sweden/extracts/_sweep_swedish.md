# Swedish-sources sweep: Betänken Eder! (Stockholm 1904)

Sweep date: 2026-10-24. Sources: Svenska tidningar (tidningar.kb.se, free for pre-1906 material), LIBRIS, Projekt Runeberg, KB's IIIF page images, one American 1904 reprint on archive.org. Every newspaper item below was found through the Svenska tidningar full-text search; the short quotes are read from the page images (KB IIIF) or, where marked "snippet", from the search engine's OCR snippet. OCR snippets can contain misreadings; image-read quotes were checked by eye. Viewer links have the form `https://tidningar.kb.se/<id>/part/<n>/page/<n>`.

Labels: **FOUND** = stated in a source quoted below. **INFERRED** = my reasoning from found material, not stated anywhere.

## Answer in brief

- **FOUND, and against the claim:** No 1904 source names Svenska freds- och skiljedomsföreningen (SFSF) or any other organisation as the initiator, financer or distributor of *Betänken Eder!*. All contemporary notices name only the publisher, Björck & Börjesson, and describe a commercial operation: trade discounts, sale-or-return, adverts in the Social Democratic and labour press, colporteurs and cigar shops.
- **FOUND:** The first edition, 10,000 copies, came out on the afternoon of **Tuesday 5 July 1904**. That was eight days after The Times (27 June). Further printings followed: the 10th–15th thousand by 9 July, 35,000 copies in two weeks by 24–26 July (more than 20,000 of them in Stockholm), and the Runeberg copy is the "31:sta–35:te tusendet".
- **FOUND:** The Swedish text was not made for the pamphlet. The same translation, with the same Swedish chapter titles, was printed in full four days earlier in **Göteborgs Handels- och Sjöfartstidning (GHT), Friday 1 July 1904**, part B, pages 7–8. Within days, a dozen provincial papers reprinted the GHT text.
- **FOUND:** GHT's text includes **chapter IX** ("»Hvad skola vi göra, då kriget nu ändå börjat?»"), the Mark 1:15 epigraph, the sailor's postscript and the datelines. The Björck & Börjesson pamphlet drops chapter IX, the epigraphs, the postscript and the datelines, but advertised itself as a "fullständig öfversättning" (complete translation).
- **INFERRED:** Björck & Börjesson took GHT's translation, probably without credit, and cut it to 40 pages. Nothing found shows who made the cuts or why.
- **FOUND:** The translator is unnamed everywhere: in the pamphlet, in GHT, in all reprints and in five LIBRIS records. LIBRIS codes the original language as Russian (`041 $h rus`). That is a cataloguing convention, not a statement about the actual source. **INFERRED:** GHT's subtitle ("bref i världsbladet Times") points to translation from the English Times text.
- **FOUND:** The slogan "Till krig emot kriget!" appears with the pamphlet from its first press notices (8 July). The phrase was common Swedish peace-movement and socialist vocabulary long before 1904: it is in the press from 1880, in the SFSF-era paper *Fredsvännen* in 1889, in W. T. Stead's *War against War* (1899), and on socialist placards. **INFERRED:** it was a stock slogan the publisher put on the cover. Nothing ties it specifically to SFSF.
- **FOUND:** SFSF's 1904 chair was **med. dr N. A. Nilsson, Örebro**, re-elected in January 1904 and again in January 1905. Its paper *Fredsfanan* was edited until late 1904 by **Carl Sundblad**, the society's *missionsföreståndare* (head of its travelling outreach). During 2–6 July 1904, the week the pamphlet appeared, Nilsson, Sundblad and other Swedish peace leaders were at the 5th Nordic Peace Meeting in Copenhagen. Reports of the central board's meeting on the way home (6 July) do not mention Tolstoy.
- **FOUND:** *Fredsfanan* no. 5 (September 1904) listed its contents in the press. They included the Copenhagen meeting, "Rysk-Japanska kriget och Sverges neutralitet" and "Ett billigt och godt försvar" ("A cheap and good defence"). Tolstoy is not among them.
- **Verdict:** The SFSF-initiative claim is **not confirmed and is contradicted by the available evidence**. The pamphlet was a fast commercial reprint by a Stockholm publisher with a radical, Russia-critical list. It became no. 1 of that publisher's own series *Fria ord*, built on a translation first published by the Liberal Göteborg daily GHT.

## Chapter IX omission

(a) **The English text.** FOUND: the American Peace Society reprint "Reprinted from the London Times, June 27, 1904. Translated by V. Tchertkoff … and I. F. M." (Boston 1904; https://archive.org/details/bethinkyourselv00socigoog, text file `_djvu.txt`) contains chapter IX under the head "HOW ACT NOW THAT WAR HAS BEGUN?". It opens "But, 'How are we to act now, immediately among ourselves in Russia? …'". It has English chapter heads for chapters II–XII and closes the main text "13 May, 1904." before the sailor postscript (headed "Rejoicing at the wholesale destruction of lives"). Its heads are:
   - II: "Excuses by which the war is justified"
   - III: "As if Jesus had never existed and taught"
   - IV: "Replies of soldiers, diplomats and journalists"
   - V: "On the edge of a precipice"
   - VI: "The only remedy"
   - VII: "True religion needed"
   - IX: "How act now that war has begun?"
   - X: "Love your enemies and you will have none"
   - XI: "The slaughter and crimes of the present war"
   - XII: "New and newer victims"

   **Not checked:** whether these heads stood in The Times itself or were added in Boston (the Times Digital Archive was not consulted). The Free Age Press popular edition was not found online in this sweep.

(b) **Where the Swedish text and chapter titles came from.** FOUND: GHT on 1 July 1904 (Evidence 5) printed the full text with **13** numbered chapters, each with a Swedish title. These are the titles the pamphlet uses, including chapter IX "»Hvad skola vi göra, då kriget nu ändå börjat?»". GHT splits Tolstoy's chapter XI in two: "XI. Man ämnar offra ytterligare 'bara' 50,000 lif" and "XII. Huru nationerna uppfostras till att slakta hvarandra". Its chapter XIII "»Hvart kan man komma undan?»" is Tolstoy's XII. So the pamphlet's 12 titled sections are GHT's 13 minus chapter IX, with the numbers removed. That accounts for "eleven chapters" under twelve titles.

   INFERRED: GHT made its own titles. Most are key sentences lifted from the chapter itself: VII "Det största onda, hvaraf nutidens människor lida" is the chapter's first sentence, and VI "»Innan jag är kejsare, är jag Guds arbetare»" is a quotation inside it. Two resemble the American heads: V "precipice" ~ "afgrund", and IX "How act now that war has begun?" ~ "Hvad skola vi göra, då kriget nu ändå börjat?". GHT may have had an English text with heads in front of it, but most of its titles do not translate them.

   FOUND: Socialdemokraten's serial (from 2 July) used numbered "KAPITEL" heads and a different opening translation. Provincial papers reprinted GHT's version, chapter IX included (Evidence 7–8), and a free-church daily gave chapter IX on its own (Evidence 15).

(c) **Who cut IX, and why.** FOUND: only the Björck & Börjesson pamphlet lacks chapter IX. Its 5 July press release still called it a "fullständig öfversättning". No source explains the cut.

   INFERRED, with possibilities only:
   - It saved space for a 40-page format at 25 öre, since the postscript, datelines and epigraphs are also gone.
   - It removed the chapter most open to a charge of inciting refusal of military service under Swedish law in 1904.
   - It suited a buyership of Social Democrats and liberals who were critical of the Tsar but not of national defence as such.

   On SFSF's position: its September 1904 *Fredsfanan* carried "Ett billigt och godt försvar" and an article on Swedish neutrality (Evidence 20). This fits the society's arbitration-and-defence line, but nothing links SFSF to the pamphlet's editing. **Nothing found supports the idea that SFSF asked for chapter IX to be cut.**

## Context

- **Russia and Finland, June 1904 (FOUND):** "Bobrikoff skjuten!" (Norrbottens Allehanda 1904-06-16; https://tidningar.kb.se/q6qm2zltnxtgh4ms/part/1/page/1). The Finnish governor-general was shot in the Senate in Helsinki on 16 June and died that night. The pamphlet appeared three weeks later. Its back-cover list (Stepniak) and its follow-up *Tsaren i ny belysning* place it in the Swedish anti-Tsarist mood of that summer.
- **The press (FOUND):** Within a week the letter was summarised in nearly every Swedish paper. It was serialised by the Liberal GHT, the Social Democratic Socialdemokraten and Arbetarbladet, the free-church Svenska Morgonbladet and many provincial papers, and printed whole as a Dalarna supplement. Some conservative papers questioned it. For example, Trelleborgs Nyhets- och Annonstidning (1904-07-20, snippet) called it "den lögnaktiga historien »Betänken Eder»", on the claim that The Times had made it up.
- **SFSF in mid-1904 (FOUND):**
  - a Riksdag dispute in spring 1904 over a 2,500 kr grant for representation at the Nordic meeting, settled in a joint vote of 234 to 128 (Jämtlandsposten 1904-04-08, snippet; which side won not read)
  - the 5th Nordic Peace Meeting in Copenhagen, 2–6 July
  - a delegate to the Boston congress
  - a petition to the King in September 1904 against shooting drill in elementary schools (Lunds Dagblad 1904-09-02, snippet)
  - internal conflict over *Fredsfanan* in late 1904, and a split in September 1905
- **The 1899 "Letter to the Swedes" (FOUND, Swedish side):** Göteborgsposten 1899-02-17 (snippet) says Tolstoy's long letter answered "en samfäld skrifvelse" from "flera personer i Stockholm och Upsala" on raising refusal of military service at the Hague conference. Folktidningen 1899-03-04 says it was printed in Aftonbladet. Neither names SFSF. The corpus extracts in this folder (`v90_367_368_…`) give the PSS commentary: a group of Swedish intelligentsia.

## Leads not followed up

- **The Times, 27 June 1904** (Times Digital Archive): did it print chapter heads, and is GHT's text a translation of it? Compare also the Free Age Press "popular edition" and the Hedemora supplement (https://digitalt.kb.se/dark-23760337).
- **Who translated for GHT:** check GHT's own columns of 30 June–2 July for an editorial note, and GHT's 1904 editorial staff. Also the Motala Tidning item of 1904-07-04 p. 3, whose snippet reads "… öfversättning, följande brottstycken", may credit GHT.
- **Björck & Börjesson:** partners' names, links to the Social Democratic press (they advertised constantly in Socialdemokraten and Brand), and whether they paid GHT.
- ***Fredsfanan* 1904** is not in Svenska tidningar. The issue for July/August (no. 4 appeared late June, before the letter) needs a library copy (KB or Uppsala). Also Carl Sundblad's history of the society 1883–1903 (vol. II 1904; noted in Öresundsposten 1905-08-29) and the SFSF archive (location not verified in this sweep).
- **Socialdemokraten's serial 2–9 July:** read all instalments to see whether it printed chapter IX and where it switched to GHT wording.
- **Hvar 8 dag (Göteborg) 1904-07-10, pp. 5 and 7:** article "»Betänken eder!»" (not read).
- Svensk bokkatalog 1901–1905 was not checked.
- **Arnoldson's own reaction in 1904:** Arnoldson was no longer chair. Search "Arnoldson Tolstoy 1904" in Svenska tidningar.
