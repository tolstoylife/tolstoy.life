## 2 — On "cost" — economic words carrying a money sense

> perhaps "how it changed his family life" or something like that. The word "cost" is something we
> equate to money even though it can mean other things.

Johan's worked example: the common Swedish Lord's Prayer uses *skuld* ("debt"), which most Swedes
hear as a **monetary** debt even though it also means *sin* — so he says *synd* ("sin") instead. His
point: a society's base assumptions get smuggled in through economic vocabulary, with
property / copyright / economy made to feel like the natural ground of things.

This is the same mechanism as the **"words as a weapon"** thread in the
[crisis dive's annotations](../crisis/annotations.md): word-choice quietly reframes. It points two
ways:

- **For the dive's prose** — see **Dive edits applied**. For the record: English "cost" also carries
  a strong *non-monetary* sense of sacrifice ("it cost him his marriage"); the money-reading is
  sharper for a Swedish ear, which is exactly the kind of cross-language slippage worth catching.
- **For the wiki** — Tolstoy himself attacks this naturalising of money/property; it is the spine of
  the [copyright-renunciation dive](../copyright-renunciation/index.html). When ingesting the
  gospel / property cluster, keep the economic vocabulary *visible as a choice*, not naturalised.

### Johan's deeper steer (2026-06-05): the cost runs the other way

Asked how far to reword, Johan turned the frame around — in Tolstoy's (and the Gospels') accounting
the "cost" points the *opposite* way to the worldly reading:

> Being attached to this world is costing you [your] soul and the Kingdom of God. "Blessed are the
> poor that shall inherit the earth." Becoming a follower of Christ means the world hate you. "What
> is [it] to gain the world if you lose your soul?" It is very costly to not be able to renounce
> property. I would like tolstoy.life to be Leo['s] views. At the same time I don't want tolstoy.life
> to flip the arch-enemy from Chertkov to Sophia. We understand that the process of giving up the
> world is very hard and goes against everything you've been taught.

The load-bearing instruction, in three parts:

1. **Centre Leo's value-inversion.** "What it cost at home" silently keeps the world's ledger, where
   the comfortable household is the asset and the gospel work the expense. Tolstoy's ledger is the
   reverse — attachment to property and status is the true cost, paid in soul. The household conflict
   is the *division Christ foretells* for anyone who follows him ("not peace but a sword"; "a man's
   foes shall be they of his own household" — Matt. 10:34–36, which Tolstoy harmonised), not a
   domestic melodrama. That is why the published prose drops "cost" for the neutral "strain": it
   names the real friction without valorising the worldly household as the thing being spent.
2. **Do not flip the villain to Sofia.** Complicating the Sofia-as-victim frame must never harden
   into a Sofia-as-antagonist frame. Renouncing the world is genuinely hard and runs against
   everything one is raised to want; her resistance is that universal difficulty, met with sympathy,
   not blame. (Note 4 already guards this; the wiki Sofia page must keep it.)
3. **The site speaks from Leo's view.** Default to Tolstoy's own frame and vocabulary; mainstream /
   worldly framings are read critically, never adopted as the baseline.

(A further private socio-economic aside Johan marked "nothing that should be published" is withheld
here at his request.)

## 4 — On the ambivalence framing (Sofia-as-victim / Chertkov-as-villain)

> Good framing. For the same reasons as his great novels are believable: you can identify with both
> good and bad characters.

Johan ties the dive's refusal of the *Last Station* melodrama to Tolstoy's own novelistic method —
the even-handedness that makes Anna or Karenin believable is what makes the *primary record* of the
marriage believable when it declines to name a villain. He paired it with a line Standard Ebooks had
just quoted:

> "Character determines action," she said, slowly, at last. "That is the secret of the great
> novelists. They put themselves behind and within their characters, and so make us feel that every
> act of their personages is not only natural but even — given the conditions — inevitable."
> — quoted by Standard Ebooks (@standardebooks.org), 5 Jun 2026

— and one counter-observation about Tolstoy's *self*-portrayal:

> Tolstoy['s] early childhood series was too flattering of himself.

For ingestion: the believable-because-ambivalent reading belongs on the Sofia / marriage pages —
frame the diaries as a two-sided primary record, not a brief for either party. But carry Johan's
caveat: the even-handedness Tolstoy extends to his *characters* he did **not** always extend to
*himself* — the early autobiographical trilogy (*Childhood / Boyhood / Youth*) idealises its
narrator. Self-presentation is not the novelist's god's-eye fairness; date the difference rather than
crediting Tolstoy with lifelong self-honesty.

## 5 — On "harmony" (the term, and into Swedish)

> harmony — Is the word used to describe the process of harmonising the gospels?

Yes. "Harmony" / "gospel harmony" is the established technical term, not the dive's coinage:

- **Process vs. product.** The *act* is to *harmonize* (noun *harmonization*); the *product / genre*
  is *a harmony* — a single continuous narrative woven from the four gospels. The archetype is
  Tatian's 2nd-c. *Diatessaron* ("through four"); the line runs through Augustine and Osiander's
  *Harmonia evangelica* (1537).
- **Tolstoy's own editions used it** — *The Four Gospels **Harmonised** and Translated*
  (Brotherhood / Walter Scott, 1895–96) and *…**Harmonized**…* (Wiener, 1904).
- **But his own word distances him from the tradition.** Tolstoy's Russian is «Соединение» — a
  *joining into one* — not *гармония*; and §3 of the dive has him dismissing the chronological
  harmonists (Farrar, Reuss, Grechulevich) as "unsuccessful," because he joins by *meaning*, not
  history. The genre label fits; his framing resists being filed beside them.

**Swedish.** The genre term is **evangelieharmoni**; the act is *harmonisera* / *sammanställa* /
*förena … till ett*. Tolstoy's «соединение» maps to *förening / sammanfogning / sammanställning*, not
*harmoni* — the same distance the Russian keeps from *гармония*.

### Into Swedish — the reception (research note, 2026-06-06)

Could there be a Swedish translation? Checked against Project Runeberg, the Svenskt översättarlexikon
and Libris:

- **The full harmony — apparently never.** No standalone Swedish edition of *Соединение и перевод
  четырёх Евангелий* surfaced; it is absent from Runeberg and the översättarlexikon Tolstoy
  work-list. Consistent with its censored, forbidding status (even in English it was completed whole
  only once, Wiener 1904).
- **The condensed strand did reach Swedish**, mostly through **Walborg Hedberg** (Bonnier), Tolstoy's
  principal Swedish translator from the Russian:
  - ***Anden af Kristi lära: en kommentarie öfver evangeliets mening*** — Bonnier, Stockholm,
    **1891** (Libris-confirmed; KB holds a digitised copy). The nearest Swedish equivalent to the
    gospel project — "the spirit of Christ's teaching: a commentary on the meaning of the gospel."
    **Very likely the Gospel-in-Brief strand, but the catalogues do not certify the Russian
    original** — the digitised KB copy could settle it.
  - *Hvari min tro består* (1887) = *What I Believe* / «В чём моя вера?»
  - *Jesu lära: enkelt framställd* (Geber, 1909, Hanny Flygare); *Kristi lära förklarad för barnen*
    (1910, Hedberg); *Religiösa skrifter* (1927, Ellen Rydelius).

For ingestion: this seeds a **"Tolstoy's gospel work in Swedish / Scandinavian reception"** thread
(also a `notCovered` line in `dossier.yaml`). Worth carrying — the Swedish titles reach for *lära*
and *evangeliets mening* rather than *evangelieharmoni*, independently landing where Tolstoy's
«соединение» points, away from the harmonist genre.

Sources: Svenskt översättarlexikon (Verk av Tolstoj; Walborg Hedberg); Project Runeberg (Tolstoy);
Libris (*Anden af Kristi lära*; *Jesu lära*).
