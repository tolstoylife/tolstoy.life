# Bethink Yourselves! — translation ledger

Whose English each part of `bethink-yourselves.en-restored.md` is, and why. The tolstoy.life English is made in two stages. Stage 1 (2026-11-22, this table) keeps the 1904 English of V. G. Chertkov and I. F. Mayo and translates in only what it lacks: the epigraphs, the passages Chertkov changed at proof stage (undone as in the restored Russian), and the PSS text the 1904 English left out. Stage 2 will go through the 1904 English against the restored Russian and replace it only where it softens, drifts or leaves out words; each replacement gets a row here with the Russian, the 1904 wording, ours, and which of the three it is.

Every addition is marked against the 1904 English, so the "Editorial changes" switch shows it in green; a 1904 wording that is replaced shows struck through. A script confirmed that the file, with every addition dropped, every struck-through wording put back and the new notes removed, is exactly `bethink-yourselves.en-1904.md`.

## Open questions (for Stage 2, or for Johan)

- **English-language sources not yet compared with their originals:** Channing, Swift, Savage, Crosby, Fluegel. Tolstoy's Russian was translated, as the plan asks; a note is still due wherever his version differs in substance from the original. Swift's (Gulliver, part IV, ch. 5) agrees in substance.
- **Maude's 1937 English** (*Recollections and Essays*) prints the epigraphs; not yet compared line by line with ours.
- **Savage, VII:** the Russian has «христианское исследование» ("a Christian study"); "scientific" would fit the argument better. Check Savage's English.
- **Letourneau, II:** the Russian says «лучшее, чем ваше, человечество» ("a humanity better than yours"); translated as printed, though "ours" reads more naturally.
- **Molinari, I:** "110 million in time of war" as printed; probably a slip for 10 or 11 million.
- **Richet, IV:** "1856" as printed for the start of the Crimean War (1854).
- **Origen, VIII, second epigraph:** the commentary's Russian reads «поощряем народы к нарушению союзов» ("we encourage the nations to break their alliances"), which makes no sense as printed; translated as the devils who urge the nations to do so, as in Origen (*Against Celsus* VIII.73). Check against a manuscript image if one turns up.
- **Chapter V, Hardouin:** the PSS e-texts print «Hardouin.» after the mother's letter, so the source line covers both quotations; the eyewitness passage is Hardouin's (see alignment-notes).
- **The commentary's lower-case «бог»** in the restored Origen passages becomes "God" in English, as English writes it.
