# Bethink Yourselves!

An essay of 1904, written in the first months of the Russo-Japanese War and subtitled "a letter to the men of war and the tsar." Its Russian title is *Bethink Yourselves!* («Одумайтесь!»); the Swedish translation of the same year was titled *Betänken Eder!*. Source text: <abbr title="Полное собрание сочинений — the 90-volume Jubilee edition of the complete works">PSS</abbr> Tom 36, pp. 100–148.

The essay asks soldier, officer, diplomat, journalist and tsar in turn why they prepare to kill, and finds that each answers with an abstraction — faith, fatherland, oath. Against every outward cure, from peace conferences to revolution, it sets one: that each person stop and ask who he is and what his life is for — the "bethinking" of the title. Banned in Russia, it was first published abroad, in *The Times* of London and by Chertkov's Free Age Press, so its translations are its publication history.
